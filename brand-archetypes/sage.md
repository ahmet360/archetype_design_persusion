## Overview

The Sage archetype is driven by a desire for **truth and understanding**. Sages believe that knowledge sets people free, and they want to help others see the world clearly. The Brand Archetypes website, which calls this archetype "The Guru" (also known as the Sage), gives its core desire as "The truth will set you free!" and says these brands help customers make smarter decisions through study, analysis and objectivity (Brand Archetypes, n.d.). Sage brands promise their audience the chance to feel wise, informed and in the know.

## Key characteristics

- **Knowledgeable and expert:** Sage brands build trust through demonstrated expertise. They provide information that customers can rely on.
- **Thoughtful and analytical:** They use data, facts and careful reasoning instead of hype, and they help people tell fact from fiction.
- **Educational and guiding:** They inform and mentor their audience instead of simply selling, and they encourage people to think critically for themselves.

You can often spot a Sage brand by its focus on **sharing knowledge**. It positions itself as a teacher or guide, and it gives the audience insight before it asks for anything in return.

## Brand or website example

**[TED](https://www.ted.com/)**

TED is a clear example of the Sage archetype. Its tagline, **"Ideas worth spreading,"** frames the brand as a sharer of knowledge, not a seller of products. Its website is a library of talks from researchers, scientists and thinkers, which visitors can browse and learn from for free. The brand's authority comes from the expertise of its speakers, and its purpose is to help people understand the world better.

## Application to web design

- **Layout:** Use a clear hierarchy, generous white space and a structure that is easy to scan. Organize content into well-labeled categories so visitors can find and explore information without effort.
- **Content and tone:** Lead with substance. Use in-depth articles, evidence, citations and a calm, measured voice that explains instead of pushing a sale. Credit experts and sources so the content feels credible.
- **Typography:** Choose highly readable type, such as a clean serif or sans-serif with comfortable line spacing, that supports long-form reading. Legible, restrained type signals clarity and intelligence.
