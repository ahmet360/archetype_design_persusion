## Overview

Memphis Design is a playful, colorful design style associated with the Memphis Group, an Italian collective of designers founded in Milan in 1981 and led by Ettore Sotsass (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). The group made furniture, lamps, ceramics and textiles that rejected the plain, functional look of modernism in favor of bold color, geometric shapes and decoration. It is considered postmodernist because it deliberately breaks modernist rules such as "form follows function" and "less is more", and it treats design as expressive, humorous and emotional.

## Key characteristics

- **Bright, clashing colors:** Saturated hues such as pink, teal, yellow and purple are combined on purpose, often with black and white, to create energy rather than harmony.
- **Geometric shapes:** Simple forms such as triangles, circles, squiggles and zigzags are used as both structure and decoration.
- **Playful rejection of modernist rules:** Decoration, personality and humor are placed ahead of strict function and neutrality.

You can often spot Memphis Design by its loud color combinations, its cartoon-like geometry and its deliberately unserious attitude.

## Brand or website example

**[Figma](https://www.figma.com/)**

Figma's branding borrows the Memphis idea that simple geometric shapes and bright color can carry personality. Its logo is built from basic shapes in a set of bright, saturated colors, and its site uses abstract geometric illustrations and purple, pink and teal accents on a clean, modular card layout. The overall site is more restrained than true Memphis design, with plenty of white space and a professional tone, so the style appears mainly in its accents and illustrations. That balance shows how a modern brand can use Memphis-style playfulness without losing usability.

## Application to web design

- **Layout:** Use playful card and block layouts, with modules of different colors and sizes, to present features and content. Use floating geometric shapes as section dividers to separate areas of the page and add rhythm.
- **Typography:** Mix sizes, weights and angles in headlines to give them personality and make them feel expressive.
- **Color:** Use bright, clashing palettes on purpose, choosing a small set of saturated colors that stand out against each other.
- **Imagery:** Add animated or interactive shapes, such as floating or hover-reactive graphics, to bring the style to life. Use them sparingly so they don't distract.
- **Content and tone:** Write short headlines with personality that match the energy of the visuals.
- **User decisions:** The style creates a memorable, distinctive brand and appeals to creative and younger audiences. Color-coded blocks help users tell sections and actions apart.
- **Accessibility:** Provide meaningful alternative text for illustrations and decorative shapes, so users of assistive technology get the same information.
