## Overview

Bauhaus was a German school of design, architecture and applied arts that operated from 1919 to 1933 (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). Founded by Walter Gropius, it set out to bring together fine art, craft and industrial technology so that well-designed, functional objects could be produced for everyone. Its teaching and visual language went on to shape modern graphic, product and interface design.

## Key characteristics

- **Geometric shapes and primary colors:** Circles, squares and triangles are paired with red, yellow and blue, plus black and white.
- **Unity of art, craft and technology:** Design is treated as a blend of artistic vision, skilled making and industrial production, not as separate disciplines.
- **Sans-serif typography and grid layouts:** Clean sans-serif type is set on structured, often asymmetric grids, so the layout itself communicates hierarchy.

You can often spot Bauhaus design by its lack of ornament. Every shape, color and line has a clear purpose.

## Brand or website example

**[Apple](https://www.apple.com/)**

Apple's website and products reflect Bauhaus thinking. Pages use a restrained, grid-based layout, a clean sans-serif typeface, large flat areas of space and color, and very little decoration. Navigation is limited to a few clearly labeled links, and each product page is organized around what the product does. Apple's approach is often linked to the functionalist tradition that grew out of Bauhaus, which treats good design as simple, useful and free of unnecessary detail.

## Application to web design

- **Navigation:** Keep the menu minimal. Offer a few clearly labeled, functional links and leave out anything that is purely decorative.
- **Typography:** Use a geometric sans-serif for headings and body text. Set body copy with a readable line length and comfortable spacing, so legibility comes before style.
- **Color:** Divide sections with bold, flat blocks of color and avoid gradients, textures and shadows.
- **Imagery:** Use simple geometric shapes as graphics, and leave out decorative ornament. Every visual element should serve a purpose.
- **Content and user decisions:** Put function first and organize content around what users need to do. Reduce the number of choices on each screen so decisions are quicker.
- **Accessibility:** Keep contrast high and follow accessibility guidelines, which reflects the Bauhaus aim of design that works for everyone.
- **Consistency:** Build the site from a small set of reusable components, as a design system, echoing the Bauhaus interest in standardized, repeatable production.
